2021 “ShuWei Cup” International Contest — Problem B
Quantitative Analysis and Prediction of Extreme Precipitation:
From One City’s Record to a National Risk Map and an Investment
Portfolio
Team # 2021B-PRACTICE
Summary
The July 2021 Zhengzhou storm delivered 449 mm in 72 h and 201.9 mm in one hour, killed 398
people and caused 120.06 billion CNY of direct loss in Henan. One pipeline answers the five
tasks: an archive, a hazard layer, a held-out forecast experiment, a type-and-loss decomposition
and an investment model.
Data. The official GSOD attachment and 35 GHCN-Daily stations are merged; keeping years
with 300 valid days leaves 25 cities with 45+ years; at Zhengzhou the sources correlate at
0.6776 daily, 0.9217 annually.
Model I (trend, rarity, waterlogging). Zhengzhou’s annual total (+1.19 mm per decade, p =
0.92) and one-day maximum (+0.45 mm, p = 0.82) are flat, but wet days fall by 2.0 per decade
(p = 0.021) while intensity rises by 0.23 mm (p = 0.062): fewer, heavier events without a
change point (p = 0.88). A GEV fit (ξ = −0.026) gives 78.0 and 163.4 mm at 2 and 50 years;
the 20 July 188.7 mm has a 54-year empirical and 151-year fitted return period, the three-day
313.4 mm 6.4×103 yr. SCS-CN over 1 180 km2 yields 3.13×108 m3 of runoff; the peak hour
leaves 1.4×108 m3 above capacity and the three-day runoff needs 7.4 h to drain.
Model II (national map). Across 25 cities and one declared family of 146 controlled tests only
2 total and 1 extreme trends survive, against 7 intensity and 9 wet-day: redistribution, not more
rain. k-means separates 17 continental (602 mm, +0.80 mm per decade) from 8 humid-south
cities (1 389 mm, +3.03 mm).
Model III (who breaks a record next). Over 5–7 held-out years the target beats the distribu-
tion: the expected maximum instead of the T-year quantile cuts RMSE from 64.8 to 55.7 mm
(bootstrap gain [3.3,13.6]) and bias from −41.5 to −20.5 mm; a trend-shifted GEV reaches
53.9 mm but ranks worse, and extreme-value models beat machine learning on ranking (4/5
versus 3/5 top-five overlap). Index-flood pooling is rejected for the country (H1 = 2.56) and
accepted for two L-moment groups (H1 = 0.22, 0.03): pooling buys at most 1.2 mm, and only
group-wise. By ten-year exceedance probability the leaders are Fuzhou, Haikou, Guangzhou,
Hohhot; Zhengzhou is sixteenth.
Model IV (are the storms the same?). No: Zhengzhou is an intensity-type storm (134-year
daily event, 52% of the event total in one day), Taiyuan a persistent-type storm (9.5-year daily
event, one-day share at its 6th climatological percentile), separated by 14.1 in period ratio.
Area-normalised, Henan lost 22.4 times more than Shanxi while rainfall was only 2.2 times
larger; the identity splits that gap into density (×2.68), affected share (×2.95) and loss per
affected person (×2.84), and Shapley shares of 31/34/33/2.5% show that removing any one
social factor still leaves a factor of 8.1–8.9.
Model V (what to build). Uniform upgrading never pays: the best ratio on a 378-point menu
is 0.28 (Guangzhou), 0.18 (Haikou) and 0.10 (Zhengzhou), because damage is carried by over-
flow volume and a city-wide millimetre of capacity costs about a billion CNY. Protecting the
2% most exposed area, where damage density is twenty times the average, returns 4.05, 2.62
and 1.50 for the same money. A CVaR95 cap forces spending but buys no ratio above 0.28, so
the cap is a constraint to be met at least cost, and the least-cost way is the hotspot.
Keywords: extreme precipitation; Mann–Kendall trend; generalized extreme value; L-moments;
expected maximum; SCS-CN; false discovery rate; benefit–cost analysis; CVaR

1
Introduction
1.1
Background
On 20 July 2021 the city of Zhengzhou received 201.9 mm of rain in a single hour, the largest
hourly total ever recorded in mainland China, and 449 mm in three days against an annual nor-
mal of 640.8 mm quoted in the task statement. The storm killed 398 people in Henan province
(380 of them in Zhengzhou) and caused 120.06 billion CNY of direct economic loss, 40.90 bil-
lion of it in Zhengzhou alone, according to the State Council disaster investigation report [1].
Two and a half months later, an equally unusual but dynamically different storm parked over
Shanxi province for four days (3–6 October 2021). The provincial average of four-day rainfall
reached 120 mm, exceeding the previous record of 104 mm set in 1981, yet the province lost
5.03 billion CNY and 18 lives, an order of magnitude less than Henan [2, 3]. The contrast is
the scientific core of this problem: the same word, “extreme precipitation”, covers events that
differ in duration, concentration and consequence, and a city that prepares for one type may be
defenceless against the other.
1.2
Restatement of the tasks
The contest asks five linked questions.
1. Using the three Zhengzhou-area station records, characterise the inter-annual variability of
precipitation, identify the wet years, and quantify the July 2021 waterlogging event.
2. Collect multi-decadal precipitation series for additional cities, document their provenance
and acquisition, and analyse their trends.
3. Using the collected city data, predict which cities are likely to experience extreme precipi-
tation in the future by several different methods, and compare their predictive performance.
4. Decide whether the Zhengzhou July 2021 storm and the Shanxi October 2021 storm share
the same characteristics, and explain the difference in losses.
5. Propose a long-term urban construction plan against extreme precipitation, with a deep
analysis of typical cities.
1.3
Our work
We treat the five tasks as one measurement problem: hazard, exposure, vulnerability and in-
tervention must be expressed in the same units before any policy statement is credible. The
contributions are:
• a reproducible data pipeline that merges the official GSOD attachment with 35 GHCN-
Daily stations, decodes both provider conventions and validates the two sources against
each other on the event of interest (Section 3);
• a trend and change-point diagnosis of Zhengzhou that separates total, frequency and inten-
sity and refuses to declare a trend where the evidence is weak (Section 4);
• a false-discovery-rate controlled national trend map with a data-driven regional classifica-
tion (Section 5);

• a held-out forecast experiment in which ten strategies are compared on identical folds, and
in which we show that the definition of the predictand matters more than the choice of

2
Assumptions and notation
2.1
Assumptions
1. Station records represent their cities. One GHCN station represents one city; Chinese
sites are urban or airport stations, so they describe the built-up area rather than the whole
prefecture. The Zhengzhou station’s three-day total is 70% of the official areal 449 mm;
station data drive every statistic and design depth, and the official figure enters once, as the
scale anchor of the damage function, with the consequence of using the station total instead
reported in Section 9.
2. Valid years are homogeneous years. A year enters only with at least 300 valid precipita-
tion days; partial years are discarded rather than rescaled, because rescaling would fabricate
a seasonal distribution.
3. The baseline is stationary. The hazard model is stationary with an optional linear trend in
location, supported by the failure of the Pettitt test at Zhengzhou [4] and by the scarcity of
significant total-rainfall trends nationally.
4. Precipitation is the only hazard driver. Surge, riverine flooding and wind are not mod-
elled; the documented damage in both events was pluvial.
5. SCS-CN is a screening tool here. Applied to a 1 180 km2 city it estimates runoff volume,
not street depth; CN is bracketed between 75 and 95 and the range is reported.
6. Official losses are authoritative and comparable. The three loss figures come from re-
ports compiled under one national statistical standard; no inflation or reporting correction
is applied.
7. Damage grows as a power law in overflow, with exponent 1.5 fixed by the observed
convexity of urban flood damage and calibrated on one event.
8. Costs are planning-grade unit costs, varied in the sensitivity analysis rather than pre-
sented as tendered prices.
The notation is collected in Table 13 in Appendix A.

(ii) GHCN-Daily. We retrieved 35 Chinese station files from the NOAA National Centers
for Environmental Information [5], using the documented access pattern https://www.ncei.
noaa.gov/data/global-historical-climatology-network-daily/access/ followed by

Quality control. A year enters the analysis only if it has at least 300 valid precipitation days.
This removes the partial years at both ends of each record and the years in which a station re-
ported only part of the season. For GHCN this rule also removes 2013–2019 for many Chinese
stations, where precipitation is reported sparsely (Beijing has 132 valid days in 2013 and 78
in 2018). The consequence is stated wherever it matters: in Section 6 the held-out window
therefore contains 5, 6 or 7 valid years per city rather than the full 14 calendar years, and the
forecast target is defined accordingly.
3.2
Cross-source validation
Because the attachment and GHCN describe the same city, their overlap is a free validation ex-
periment. On 17 085 common days in Zhengzhou the daily correlation is 0.6776 and the annual-
total correlation is 0.9217; the mean daily intensity is 2.256 mm in GSOD versus 2.038 mm in
GHCN (ratio 1.107), and 74.28% of days agree to the 0.1 mm reporting precision. The disagree-
ment is dominated by days present in one archive and missing in the other, not by conflicting
measurements: GSOD alone contains 22 May 1994 (199.9 mm), a day GHCN does not report
at all. Crucially, every day of the July 2021 event agrees exactly between the two sources (40.9,
73.9, 188.7 and 50.8 mm on 18–21 July). We therefore use GHCN as the primary series for
trend work, because it is homogeneous over the full 1951–2024 span, and the attachment for
event description and cross-checking.

3.3
Analysis sample
Thirty-five GHCN files were downloaded: five are empty placeholders for stations the archive
does not hold, thirty carry data, twenty-six clear 45 valid years in 1961–2020, and twenty-five
of those form the national panel used in Sections 5–6; Yan’an (53845) is excluded because it
belongs to neither comparison. They span the humid south (Guangzhou, Haikou, Fuzhou, Nan-
chang, Hangzhou, Nanning), the Yangtze basin (Wuhan, Nanjing, Hefei), the north China plain
(Beijing, Jinan, Taiyuan, Yuncheng, Zhengzhou), the northwest (Urumqi, Lanzhou, Xining,
Yinchuan, Hohhot), the northeast (Harbin, Shenyang, Dalian) and the southwest (Kunming,
Guiyang). The four stations with data but fewer than 45 valid years (Qingdao 11, Shanghai 23,
Changsha 27, Chengdu 43) serve as context only. The panel is fixed by an explicit station list
in the code, not by the contents of the data folder.
4
Task 1: Zhengzhou’s record and the July 2021 event
4.1
Indices and trend model
We work with nine annual indices computed from the quality-controlled daily series: annual to-
tal, wet-day count (days ≥0.1 mm), simple daily intensity (SDII), maximum one-day (Rx1day)
and five-day (Rx5day) rainfall, the 95th and 99th percentile exceedance totals (R95p, R99p),
and the longest dry and wet spells. Trends are estimated with the non-parametric Sen slope
[6], tested with the Mann–Kendall statistic [7], and a change point is searched with the Pettitt
test [4] on the sign-based statistic Ut, which avoids the inflated significance that the rank-sum
variant produces on a series with a strong seasonal-free but skewed distribution.
For counts of threshold exceedances we use a Poisson generalised linear model with a log link
and a linear time term, because a count is not a continuous variable and the Sen slope on counts
is only descriptive.
4.2
Results
Table 2 reports the 1961–2020 trends. The remarkable feature is not a trend in total rainfall
but the joint behaviour of frequency and intensity: wet days fall by 2.0 days per decade (p =
0.021, robust to false-discovery control) while SDII rises by 0.23 mm dec−1 (p = 0.062) and
the number of days above 100 mm rises by 14.9% per decade (p = 0.343). The total is flat
(+1.19 mm dec−1, p = 0.92) because the increase in intensity and the decrease in frequency
offset each other. This is a textbook signature of a rainfall regime becoming more concentrated,
and it is precisely the regime in which a stationary design standard fails.

4.3
Return levels and the rarity of the event
A generalised extreme value distribution F(x) = exp{−[1 + ξ(x −µ)/σ]−1/ξ} [8] is fitted

ξ = −0.026, i.e. a light upper tail close to the Gumbel case, and Section 9 shows how little
this parameter is identified. Confidence intervals come from a parametric bootstrap with 1 000
replicates for the return levels and 500 (300 for the official 449 mm) for the event return periods,
with a fixed seed. Table 3 gives the return levels.

The difference is instructive: an event of this size was not unprecedented in the daily record
(1978 reached 189.4 mm), so a naive daily analysis would classify 2021 as rare but plausible
and stop there. The three-day total of 313.4 mm is another matter: it is the largest in 71 years of
record, and the fitted three-day GEV places it at 6.4×103 yr. For the official three-day total of
449 mm the fitted model returns 1.6×108 yr, reported only to make a point: above roughly the
200-year level the extrapolation is dominated by the uncertain shape parameter, and the interval
spans 678 yr to infinity. The honest statement is that 449 mm in 72 h lies outside the range in
which a 53-year record can discriminate between return periods; the practical conclusion does
not depend on the exact number, because the event exceeded every standard in force by a factor
of three or more.
4.4
Wet years
The five wettest years in 1961–2020, expressed as standardised anomalies, are 1964 (1 041.3 mm,
z = 2.69), 1983 (990.6 mm, z = 2.35), 2003 (953.9 mm, z = 2.10), 1984 (877.4 mm, z = 1.58)
and 1973 (819.6 mm, z = 1.19). Their spacing is irregular, consistent with the absence of a
change point: wet years are a stationary tail phenomenon, not a regime. The 2021 year, even
truncated at 5 November, reaches 1 119.4 mm (z = 3.46) and is the wettest year in the 71-year
GHCN record.
4.5
Quantifying the waterlogging event
Rainfall becomes a disaster through runoff. We estimate the storm-water volume with the SCS
curve-number method [9], Q = (P −0.2S)2/(P + 0.8S) with S = 25400/CN −254, applied
to the 1 180 km2 built-up area of Zhengzhou. Antecedent wetness in July 2021 was high, so
we bracket the curve number between 75 and 95; the central case is CN 85, appropriate to a
dense urban surface with partial sewer connection. Over the event window the areal rainfall
is 313.4 mm, giving a runoff depth of 265.4 mm and a volume of 3.13×108 m3; at the official
449 mm the volume is 4.71×108 m3. Table 4 shows the sensitivity.

The failure mechanism is a capacity mismatch in time, not in volume alone. The peak hour
delivered 201.9 mm h−1; the one-year design intensity of the Zhengzhou network is about
36 mm h−1, the two-year 43 and the three-year 50. The peak therefore exceeded design ca-
pacity by factors of 5.6, 4.7 and 4.0. Routing that hour through the same curve-number model
gives 156.6 mm of runoff, of which 120.6 mm lies above the one-year capacity — 1.4×108 m3
arriving within sixty minutes. Clearing the three-day event (313.4 mm of rain, 265.4 mm of
runoff) at full capacity takes 7.4 h at the one-year standard and 5.3 h at the three-year standard,
and during that time surface water continues to accumulate in underpasses, basements and the
metro. This is why the loss was concentrated in underground space: the network was not
merely overloaded, it was overloaded for a full working day. The daily structure of the event,
its monthly context and its return periods are plotted in Appendix E.

5
Task 2: a national trend map
5.1
Design
For each of the 25 cities we compute the same annual indices as in Section 4 and test the same
five trends. Five hypotheses on 25 cities is 125 tests, and at a nominal 5% level roughly six
would be significant by chance alone. Reporting those six as “trends” is the most common error
in this literature, so we control the false discovery rate with the Benjamini–Hochberg procedure
[10] and report only discoveries with q ≤0.05. Because the six indices are tested on the same
25 series we pool them into one family: five indices over 25 cities plus the threshold count over
the 21 cities where it is defined, i.e. 146 finite tests. Controlling each index separately over its
own cities would be less conservative and would return 1, 1, 1, 11 and 13 discoveries instead of
2, 1, 1, 7 and 9. We report the pooled family, and we also report the median Sen slope, because
the median is not distorted by the few cities with very large trends.
5.2
What actually trends
The pattern is sharply selective (Table 5). Annual totals trend in only two cities, and the one-day
maximum in one. In contrast, seven cities have a significant trend in daily intensity and nine
in the number of wet days. A national signal that appears in frequency and intensity but not in
totals is the spatial analogue of the Zhengzhou result: rainfall is being redistributed within the
year rather than increased.

5.4
Classification
To avoid imposing a geographical partition, we cluster the cities on five standardised trend
features (Rx1day, total, R95p, SDII, days ≥50 mm) plus mean intensity, with k-means and k
chosen by silhouette. The optimum is k = 2 (silhouette 0.336; k = 3 gives 0.238), and the two
clusters are geographically coherent:
• Cluster 0 (17 cities, continental): mean annual total 602 mm and mean Rx1day 63.4 mm;
per decade the Rx1day trend is +0.80 mm, the total trend −4.66 mm, the R95p trend
−4.10 mm and the count of days above 50 mm changes by −1.85%.
• Cluster 1 (8 cities, humid south): mean annual total 1 389 mm and mean Rx1day 113.8 mm;
per decade the Rx1day trend is +3.03 mm, the total trend +34.2 mm, the R95p trend
+4.36 mm and the count of days above 50 mm rises by +6.49%.
The split is not a restatement of latitude: it says that the humid south is both wetter and be-
coming more extreme, whereas the continental interior is drying in total but not becoming less
extreme per event. The city-by-index matrix, the cluster geometry and the regional anomaly
series are shown in Appendix E. This is the input to the risk ranking of Section 6.
6
Task 3: predicting which city breaks its record next
6.1
Problem formulation
The task says “predict which cities may experience extreme precipitation”. Any comparison of
methods is meaningless until the predictand is fixed, and the choice of predictand turns out to
be more consequential than the choice of model. We therefore state the problem as a genuine
forecast: using only 1961–2010, predict each city’s largest daily rainfall in the held-out window
2011–2024.
The held-out window is not the nominal 14 years. Because of the 300-valid-day rule (Sec-
tion 3), a city contributes 5, 6 or 7 test years (18 cities give 7, two give 6, three give 5);
Zhengzhou contributes 2011, 2012 and 2020–2024. The forecast horizon is therefore ntest
years per city, and every method is evaluated against the same realized maximum. Twenty-
three cities pass the training requirement of at least 40 valid years in 1961–2010 and at least 5
in 2011–2024.

6.2
Ten strategies
All strategies are fitted on the training window only.

(i) the T-year quantile with T equal to the city’s test length, fitted by maximum likelihood; (ii)
the same quantile fitted by L-moments [11], which is more stable on short records; (iii) the
expected maximum of the next n years, E[Mn] =

0 F−1(u1/n)du by adaptive quadrature, the
statistic that minimises mean squared forecast error when the predictand is a maximum and
which exceeds the n-year quantile by 0.577σ under a Gumbel tail, so a quantile target is biased
low; (iv) the expected maximum with the location shifted by the Sen slope over the 32-year
gap between window midpoints; (v) a peaks-over-threshold model with the threshold at the

6.5
Regional pooling: is one growth curve admissible?
Every prediction above rests on fifty annual maxima and a five-to-seven-year hold-out, so at-
site parameters are noisy. Index-flood pooling [12] borrows strength across sites: divide each
city’s annual maxima by its mean, pool the scaled series, fit one regional GEV by L-moments,
and multiply the growth curve back by the site index [11]. On the 23 cities the growth curve is
0.916, 1.525 and 2.138 at 2, 10 and 50 years. Whether it may be applied nationally is testable,
and the Hosking–Wallis test rejects it: with 400 simulated homogeneous regions H1 = 2.56,
H2 = −0.68, Z = −0.39, and H1 > 2 marks a heterogeneous region. Pooling still shaves the
expected-maximum error from 55.7 to 54.5 mm, but it forces one curve on every city, so the
p10 ranking drifts from the at-site one (ρ = 0.545).
The test also says what the correct pooling unit is. Grouping the cities by their own L-moment
ratios (L-CV, L-skew and the log index, k = 2) yields two groups of 12 and 11 sites (the het-
erogeneity statistic is Hosking and Wallis [13]) with H1 = 0.22 and 0.03, both comfortably
homogeneous, a growth curve of 1.566 and 1.473 at ten years, an error of 56.3 mm and a rank-
ing that stays close to the at-site one (ρ = 0.843). The honest summary is therefore narrow:
pooling buys at most 1.2 mm of level accuracy, because the error is dominated by the five
record-breaking cities, but it is admissible only group-wise, and the ranking should stay at-site.
We report the grouped curve as a short-record fallback, not as a replacement.

7.2
Are they the same type?
We formalise the comparison with three criteria, each scaled to be dimensionless:
1. Rarity ratio: RP1d(Zhengzhou)/RP1d(Taiyuan) = 14.1. A ratio above 3 means the two
events are not drawn from the same rarity class.
2. Concentration gap: |0.602−0.468| = 0.134 (0.13 at printed precision). A gap above 0.10
means the temporal profiles differ materially.
3. Duration gap: both events have a five-day wet run, so this criterion contributes 0.
Two of the three criteria fail, so the answer is no: the Zhengzhou storm is an intensity-type event
and the Shanxi storm is a persistence-type event. The distinction is not academic. Intensity-
type events are governed by the peak hour and by the capacity of drainage and of underground
space; persistence-type events are governed by cumulative volume, by slope stability and by
the saturation of reservoirs and soils. A city that has upgraded only its pipes is prepared for
neither, and a city that has prepared for a 1-in-100 year daily total may still be destroyed by a
1-in-10 year daily total that lasts four days.

8.1
From hazard to a decision variable
A construction plan is an allocation of money across measures, so the model must price risk.
We use a three-day event scale throughout, because that is the scale at which the two storms of
Section 7 caused damage.
Hazard. For each city, annual maximum three-day rainfall X is fitted with a GEV by L-
moments over 1961–2020. The design depth for a T-year standard is RL3d(T); for Zhengzhou
it is 102.9 mm at T = 2, 162.3 mm at T = 10 and 207.2 mm at T = 50, against an observed
2021 value of 313.4 mm (station) and 449 mm (official).
Two accounting conventions coexist here and answer different questions. Task 1 asks how
much water arrives in the peak window and how long the network needs to clear it; Task 5 asks
what a year of rainfall costs, so it routes the 72-hour total through the same curve-number model
and prices the resulting volume against capacity. The Task 1 peak-hour excess of 1.4×108 m3
and the Task 5 event budget of 3.50×108 m3 are therefore not interchangeable.

depth equivalent of distributed storage Ssto. Both the hazard and the overflow are measured

8.5
A three-speed construction plan
Speed 1: protect the hotspots (0–5 years). Identify the 2–5% of each city where damage
density is at least ten times the average (underpasses, metro portals, underground car parks,
hospitals, power substations) and protect exactly those with local storage, raised entrances,
sealed shafts and pumped drainage. The ratio is 1.0–4.1 across the assumed pairs, the measure
is reversible and fast, and closure protocols for underground space should be written into the
operating licence, because the Zhengzhou failure was as much a decision failure as an engi-
neering one.
Speed 2: source control where it is cheap (5–15 years). Green roofs, permeable pavement
and detention ponds reduce the curve number and the runoff volume, at 150 million CNY per
km2 of full coverage. Our grid shows that 15–30% coverage never pays on its own; it also
delivers amenity and heat benefits the model does not monetise, so it belongs where land is
cheap and redevelopment is already happening.
Speed 3: conveyance capacity only where exposure is extreme (15–30 years). Deep tunnels
and large pumping stations cost a billion CNY per millimetre of design depth over a city like
Zhengzhou; they pay only under a binding risk cap, and even there storage is the cheaper
instrument. For most cities the money is better spent on the first two speeds plus non-structural
measures.
Cross-cutting: measure the right variable. Section 6 showed that a daily-index screen misses
a Zhengzhou-type disaster entirely. Every city should therefore archive sub-hourly precipitation
and publish design storms at both a three-day and an hourly duration. The current Chinese stan-
dard (GB 50014-2021 [14], with GB 55027-2022 [15]) already defines the storm-water system
as source reduction, conveyance and detention plus non-structural measures, and sets design
return periods of 1–3 years for ordinary, 3–5 for important and 10 for especially important ar-
eas; our results say that this ladder is reasonable for conveyance, but that the binding constraint
on catastrophic loss is the unprotected hotspot, not the pipe diameter.
Staged budget. For Guangzhou, protecting 2% of the 1 400 km2 area with 5 mm of local
storage means 1.4 × 105 m3, about 112 million CNY at the unit cost used here; with sealed
entrances and operating procedures the first phase stays well below half a billion CNY, several
times less than one millimetre of city-wide capacity over the same area (1.19 billion CNY), and
it returns 4.05 times its cost in avoided damage before deaths are counted.

9
Sensitivity, strengths and weaknesses, conclusions
9.1
Sensitivity analysis
A result that flips under a plausible change of a nuisance parameter is not a result. Table 14 in
Appendix B records, for every parameter, whether it is assumed, estimated or calibrated, where
it comes from, and whether it was swept; we report here what the sweeps changed.
Curve number. The capacity ratio of 5.6 is the observed peak hour divided by the design
intensity and is therefore independent of CN by construction; it cannot be used as evidence
of robustness. What does depend on CN is the volume. Runoff from the three-day event
ranges from 2.72 to 3.52 × 108 m3 across CN 75–95, and the time needed to drain that runoff
at 36 mm h−1 from 6.4 to 8.3 h, so the statement that the network was overloaded for most of
a working day survives the whole bracket. In Task 5 the same bracket moves Haikou’s best
uniform ratio from 0.155 (CN 75) to 0.274 (CN 95): the level moves, the verdict does not.
Shape parameter and estimation method. Zhengzhou’s tail is not identified, and the windows
we use do not even agree on its sign, so every window is named. On the 1961–2020 daily
maxima the MLE gives ξ = −0.026 and the L-moment fit −0.001; on 1961–2024, −0.007
and +0.030; on the 1961–2010 training window of Task 3, −0.042 and −0.022. The spread
between estimators inside one window is as large as the spread between windows, which is
the honest summary of a 50–60 year record. The 100-year level is therefore quoted as [139.2,
242.5] mm, and the 449 mm total only as an order of magnitude (1.6×108 yr, interval 678 yr to
infinity), never as a number a planner should use. The estimator moves the Task 5 design depth
by 0.5% (102.9 mm by L-moments against 102.4 mm by MLE on 1961–2020) and Haikou’s
ratio from 0.181 to 0.179, so no conclusion rests on it.
Prediction target. The most influential choice in Task 3 is the predictand. Quantile versus
expected maximum shifts RMSE by 9.1 mm with a paired bootstrap interval of [3.3, 13.6] mm
and bias by 21 mm, more than any distributional assumption; the gain of the trend correction
over the stationary expected maximum, 1.7 mm with interval [−1.9,7.0], is inside the noise.
Both targets are reported and the expected maximum is recommended for planning.
Storm-type thresholds. The type test uses a rarity ratio of 3 and a concentration gap of 0.10,
while the observed values are 14.1 and 0.134. Relaxing the thresholds to 10 and 0.05 leaves
both criteria violated, so the “different type” verdict is robust to the threshold choice.
Investment parameters. The damage exponent and the discount rate drive the Task 5 levels.
Varying γ over 1.2–1.8 and the discount rate over 2–6% moves the best uniform ratio into 0.06–
0.44 for all three cities (Guangzhou highest, Zhengzhou lowest), so no parameter combination
on that range brings uniform capacity to break-even. Using MLE rather than L-moments for
the two-year design depth moves the ratio by less than 0.003, so the tail estimator is not what
drives the verdict. Anchoring the damage function on the station total (313.4 mm) instead of
the official areal 449 mm rescales every ratio by 2.465, which lifts Guangzhou’s hotspot ratio
to 10.0 while leaving the uniform menu at or below 0.69 — the hotspot conclusion is the robust
one.
9.2
Strengths
• Reproducibility. Every number is generated by the scripts in Appendix D from archived raw files
with fixed seeds; the pipeline decodes both provider conventions and validates them against each
other.

• Honest inference. False-discovery control over one explicitly declared family of 146 tests, bootstrap
intervals on the two comparisons that decide the paper, a heterogeneity test before any regional
pooling, and an explicit refusal to interpret return periods beyond what the record supports.
• A real forecast experiment. Task 3 is evaluated on held-out years with identical folds for ten
methods, and the comparison identifies why methods differ (target definition, bias, ranking) rather
than only which wins.
• A decision-relevant result. Hazard, exposure, vulnerability and cost share units, so the Task 5 policy
follows from the Task 1–4 measurements; protecting 2% of the area reaches 4.05 at a fraction of the
cost of a uniform upgrade that reaches 0.28.
9.3
Weaknesses and how they would be fixed
• Daily resolution. The archives are daily, so the hourly peak enters only through the official record;
this is why the daily screen ranks Zhengzhou low rather than first. Sub-hourly telemetry would fix it.
• Decomposition is an identity. The three factors in Task 4 exhaust the loss gap by construction and
do not establish causation; only the exposure mechanism is corroborated externally.
• Short held-out window. The 300-valid-day rule leaves 5–7 test years per city, so RMSE carries a
large sampling error and close calls (GEV versus Gumbel) should not be over-read.
• Loss granularity and hydraulics. Losses are provincial and municipal rather than district-level, and
the damage function is calibrated on one event; we compute volumes and capacity ratios, not street
depths. A district inventory over several events plus a coupled 1D–2D model would let the exponent
be estimated rather than assumed.
• Stationarity. The hazard model is stationary with an optional linear trend and uses no climate
projections; defensible for the baseline given the weak national trends, but a 30-year horizon should
carry a scenario range, since observed intensification of sub-daily extremes [16] points the same way
as our Zhengzhou result.
9.4
Conclusions
Task 1. Zhengzhou’s rainfall is not increasing in total; it is becoming more concentrated. Wet
days fall by 2.0 per decade (p = 0.021) while daily intensity rises, and no change point is
detectable. The July 2021 daily total (188.7 mm) was a 54-year empirical event and did not
break the 1978 record, but the three-day total (313.4 mm) was the largest in 71 years and the
official 449 mm lies beyond reliable extrapolation. The waterlogging was a capacity failure
in time: the peak hour exceeded the one-year design intensity 5.6-fold, leaving 1.4 × 108 m3
above capacity, and the three-day runoff needed 7.4 h to drain.
Task 2. Across 25 cities only 2 annual-total and 1 one-day-extreme trend survive false-discovery
control, against 7 intensity and 9 wet-day trends: redistribution, not more rain. Six belts differ
in the sign of the total trend (South +21.5, Southwest −27.1 mm per decade) while every belt
loses wet days, and two clusters capture the split (silhouette 0.336).
Task 3. The predictand matters more than the distribution: forecasting the expected maximum
of n years rather than the T-year quantile cuts RMSE from 64.8 to 55.7 mm and bias from
−41.5 to −20.5 mm. Extreme-value models dominate machine learning on ranking (top-five
overlap 4/5 versus 3/5 at best; Spearman 0.75 versus 0.64), while regression removes the bias.
Five cities broke their record in the held-out window, none in 2021, which exposes the blind
spot of daily indices for sub-daily disasters. Fuzhou, Haikou, Guangzhou and Hohhot carry the

highest ten-year exceedance probabilities; Zhengzhou ranks sixteenth on probability and tenth
on the composite.
Task 4. The two storms are different types. Zhengzhou is an intensity-type event (1-day return
period 134.5 years, 52% of the event total in one day, 201.9 mm in one hour); Taiyuan is a
persistence-type event (9.5-year return period, concentration at the 6.5th percentile of its own
climatology). Losses differ by 23.9, of which 1.07 is the area ratio; the remaining 22.4 splits
into population density (2.68), affected share (2.95) and loss per affected person (2.84), and a
Shapley decomposition assigns 31.3, 33.6 and 32.7% to the three, so removing any one still
leaves 8.1–8.9. Rainfall depth differs by only 2.2, so the gap is exposure and vulnerability,
not meteorology. Zhengzhou should be screened on hourly intensity, Taiyuan on multi-day
accumulation.
Task 5. Uniform capacity is never the efficient first purchase once damage is priced per unit of
overflow volume: across the 378-point menu the best ratio is 0.28 (Guangzhou), 0.18 (Haikou)
and 0.10 (Zhengzhou), and no social plan exceeds 0.26. Protecting the 2% most exposed area,
where damage density is twenty times the average, yields 4.05, 2.62 and 1.50 for the same
money — the reverse of the hazard ordering, because absolute damage density decides whether
a hotspot investment pays. Under a CVaR95 cap no city invests without one and every cap is
met below 0.28, so a cap is a constraint to satisfy at least cost, and the least-cost instrument is
the hotspot. The plan is therefore three-speed: hotspots and closure protocols within five years,
source control where redevelopment makes it cheap within fifteen, and large conveyance only
where exposure is extreme. Because a daily screen cannot see a Zhengzhou-type event, every
city should archive sub-hourly rainfall.
