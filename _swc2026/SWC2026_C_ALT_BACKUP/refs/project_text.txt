                                         Summary
     Background and Task An unmanned-supermarket restocking record holds 69 episodes,
66,070 timestamped frames and 184 videos. The operator must decide what to believe, when
to look again, and how to order 8–16 jobs under time and cost budgets. We treat three coupled
problems: segmenting a record into operation states with a reliability score (Q1), predicting a
hand state through missing observations and pricing a paid re-observation (Q2), and scheduling
jobs under uncertain service durations (Q3).
Modeling Framework A record is modelled with a Bayesian online change-point run-length
posterior and a hierarchical Beta–Binomial reliability layer; a damped constant-velocity Kalman
filter priced by a one-step value-of-information rule; and a scenario chance-constrained mixed-
integer program rolled forward after each decision.
Results Q1 detects 399 change points with boundary F1 0.183 at ±0.5 s and retains 95.4% of
segments at a one-sided 97.5% posterior lower bound of 0.90. Q2 cuts the masked-window error
from 21.50 cm (constant velocity) to 9.46 cm, growing as 𝐿 0.741 ; a look is priced at 1.61 cm, and
a blind two-look schedule beats both the no-look filter and a drift trigger (9.85 against 12.88 cm
at a 3.0 s gap). Q3 lifts the tightest-case weighted on-time rate from 0.522 (EDF) to 0.711, of
which 0.084 is re-planning alone, yet trails SLACK at 0.741; the ranking survives a six-level
chance-level sweep, a train-only residual bank and all five official service mappings, whose
level moves by 0.336. A trained ridge predictor reaches 8.65 cm at 1.0 s; a split-conformal layer
delivers 0.88–0.95 coverage at a nominal 0.90.
Validation and Limitation Every number is bound to a key in the data/ JSON products by
the audit script in Appendix A; the split is environment-level (16/4/4), and the Q3 residual bank
pools all 343 eligible segments across splits, a leakage bounded by a train-only ablation. Solver
fallbacks and the honest negatives behind those rankings are reported.

Key word: change-point detection, Beta–Binomial reliability, Kalman filter, value of informa-
tion, chance-constrained scheduling

1. Introduction

1.1 Background
     An unmanned supermarket has no staff to watch a shelf. Restocking is therefore observed
in recorded sessions instead of being instructed: a worker wears a head-mounted rig, the hands
carry 21 keypoints each, and the episode is stored as video plus timestamped kinematic tables.
The record is rich but not self-explanatory. A number can be complete and still unusable (a
hand occluded at the moment that matters); a segment can be long and still uninformative (the
hand rests); and a job can be finished late because the estimate of its duration was optimistic,
not because the worker was slow. The 2026 ShuWei Cup C problem asks for exactly these three
judgements: what state the record is in and how far it can be trusted, what can be said while
observations are missing, and how to schedule under duration uncertainty.
1.2 Restatement of the Problems
     Problem 1. Given three archives with different containers, define operation-state changes
and observation reliability, retain the records and intervals that can support fitting and error
evaluation, and give at least one reproducible baseline.
     Problem 2. Under a fixed masking protocol (mask window, observation budget, per-request
cost and delay fixed in advance for every method), predict the state from the information available
at the decision time, decide when to request a re-observation, and report prediction error, cost
and waiting time against last-state, motion and fixed-interval baselines.
     Problem 3. Given 8, 12 or 16 jobs with release times, due times, weights, zones and
precedences, choose order, reserved service time 𝑏, and whether to pay for extra observation, so
as to maximise completion and on-time performance subject to a cost budget and a horizon.
1.3 Our Work
     This is a deliberately different attack from the obvious one. Instead of hand-building a
feature vector and clustering it, we treat a record as a run-length posterior over kinematic change
points and put a hierarchical reliability model on top. Instead of regressing a masked window
and calibrating a fixed re-observation threshold, we run a physical state-space filter and let a
value-of-information rule choose observations. Instead of tuning a priority heuristic, we solve a
chance-constrained integer program and roll it forward. Each of the next three sections reports
one question, and Section 9 reports what happens when the assumptions are stressed.

3. Assumptions and Justifications

  1. Rigid hand. Over a 0.2 s window the keypoints move rigidly, so a hand is summarised
     by its five-fingertip centroid and that centroid’s velocity. Justification: the tables give 21
     keypoints at 30 Hz and no joint angles; used in Section 6.
  2. Bad-frame ratio as a reliability proxy. The per-record bad-frame ratio and per-segment
     bad-frame count are the only released quality signals; we treat them as noisy measurements
     of a latent segment-level usability probability, not ground truth. Justification: the statement
     calls the flags human observations of visibility, not of task success; used in Section 6.
  3. Change point as state change. An operation-state change is a change in the regime of
     the hand velocity field. Justification: the statement defines state changes operationally and
     gives no labelled state sequence; the definition is ours and is tested against the released
     annotations; used in Section 6.
  4. Duration exchangeability within family and observation prefix. Given a job family
     and an observation prefix, duration residuals are exchangeable, so a deterministic 5–95%
     quantile grid over all 343 eligible segments supplies 𝑆 = 20 scenarios, with ranks de-

throughout: boundary recovery is judged against the annotations, and reliability is judged against
the bad-frame labels, and the second does not inherit the weakness of the first.

Reliability: what it buys, and what it does not. The two-level Beta–Binomial posterior
retains 333 of 349 segments (95.4% unweighted, 95.70% duration-weighted) under the rule
𝑞 0.025 (𝜌𝑖 ) ≥ 0.90. The retention is not vacuous: of the 298 segments that contain no bad frame
it keeps all 298, and of the 51 that do it keeps 35, so the rule removes a third of the contaminated
segments while touching none of the clean ones. That is a summary of quality, and it is what
we use it for downstream.
     It is not a detector, and we say so explicitly. Asked to rank segments by whether they
contain a bad frame, the record-level posterior bound reaches AUC 0.914 and the raw record bad-
frame rate 0.915, the segment-level leave-one-out variant 0.675, and the released eligible flag
only 0.490, which is indistinguishable from chance. The record-level figures flatter the method,
because two of the record-level scores are constant inside a record: on the 21 records that contain
both a clean and a contaminated segment, and therefore the only ones where the comparison
is defined, those two scores have a within-record AUC of exactly 0.500 by construction, the
resampled record posterior bound reaches only 0.565, while the segment-level leave-one-out
score falls to 0.076, worse than chance and pointing the wrong way. The mechanism is not noise:
a contaminated segment sits in a record whose remaining segments are cleaner, so removing the
segment’s own frames from the record total lowers its prior rate and raises its usable-probability
bound, which is precisely the wrong direction. No choice of 𝜅 repairs this, because it is a property
of any leave-one-out estimator built on the record rate; breaking it needs a source of evidence
that is independent of the bad-frame labels, which this release does not provide. Question 1
therefore delivers a calibrated reliability summary and a reproducible retention rule, not a way
to pick out the segment that will fail.

7. Question 2:             Kalman Prediction and Value-of-Information Re-
    Observation

7.1 Data Insight
     The masking protocol of the statement hides a window of length 𝐿 after a 0.2 s prefix, and
the quantity to predict is the position of a representative point at the end of that window. Two
features of the corpus decide the modelling choice. First, the hand is not ballistic: the lag-1
autocorrelation of the fingertip velocity is 0.940, lag-3 is 0.897, lag-5 is 0.875 and lag-10 is
0.817, so a constant-velocity extrapolation stays competitive at the shortest mask (2.914 against
2.862 cm at 0.2 s) but breaks down once the hidden window exceeds 0.4 s. Second, the wrist
camera is present in 9 of 31 EgoDemo records and in all 14 EgoPro records but in none of the 24
EgoStandard records, and it carries no world extrinsic, so a filter that needs a second viewpoint
cannot be run on the whole corpus. Both facts point the same way: the predictor must be a
single-viewpoint recursive filter [3] with explicit velocity damping, not a ballistic extrapolation
and not a multi-view triangulation.

Model promotion. The rule was fixed before the test split was touched: a challenger replaces
the filter only if it wins at every mask length. Ridge beats the Kalman filter at every mask length

against them at comparable cost; we state that openly instead of implying a search.

Value of information. The three options the statement allows are compared in Table 9. Be-
cause the position covariance of a linear–Gaussian filter does not depend on the observations,
the marginal trace reduction of the second look equals that of the first (1158.0 and 1158.0 cm2 ),
so the optimal policy is a step function: buy none or buy both, never one. The break-even price
is 1158.0 cm2 per cost unit, equivalently 1.61 cm of root-mean-square error per look; the cen-

is averaged over windows, the trace is a covariance sum, so the two are different statistics of
the same filter), and a look is worth buying exactly when the operator values a centimetre of
accuracy more than that. This is a negative result about interior solutions and a positive result
about pricing: it converts a scheduling heuristic into a threshold an operator can argue about.

7.3 Robustness and Conclusion
     The filter is insensitive in mean error to the two noise scales (doubling 𝑞 or 𝑅 moves the
mean by less than 0.005 cm at every mask length) but very sensitive in posterior scale: doubling
𝑞 doubles the reported trace at 1.0 s (ratio 2.000) while doubling 𝑅 leaves it unchanged (ratio
1.0000). The reason is that the fitted measurement noise is already at the numerical floor (𝑅
median 1.6×10−9 ), so the filter trusts the last visible frame completely and the posterior collapses
onto it; expectation–maximisation degenerates to the same corner and was abandoned in favour
of moment matching. The reported covariance therefore understates occluded uncertainty, and
the per-record value of information collapses to one shared rule. The damping ablation is the
one that matters for accuracy: setting 𝛼 = 1 adds 12.03 cm at the longest mask.

Conclusion to Question 2.                              A single-viewpoint damped-velocity filter predicts the masked
window to 2.86 cm at 0.2 s and 9.46 cm at 1.0 s, beating constant-velocity extrapolation by a
factor of 2.3 at the long horizon. Re-observation is worth buying in pairs, never singly, and the
price at which it stops being worth buying is 1.61 cm of error per look. When a gap is long
enough for a look to land inside it, spend the looks on a blind schedule: the fixed schedule beats
both the no-look filter and a drift-triggered look at every such gap, and the trigger is statistically
indistinguishable from not looking at all.

11. Strengths and Weaknesses

11.1 Strengths
   • The three layers share one state definition, so the reliability of Q1 enters Q2 as a posterior
     variance and Q3 as a reservation, and is not re-invented in each section.
   • Every number in this paper is bound to a key in the data/ JSON products by the verifier
     script in Appendix A, which is run after each compile; the 60 cells of Table 11, the trained-
     predictor tables and the five-mapping sensitivity are checked cell by cell.
   • The scheduling layer is solved to feasibility within a 1.0 s CP-SAT limit and rolled forward,
     with solver fallbacks counted (Section 9); the prediction layer is cross-checked against three
     trained baselines with grouped cross-validation and calibrated conformal intervals, and the
     ranking is re-measured under all five service mappings against pre-declared pass marks
     (Table 13).
11.2 Weaknesses
   • The change-point layer does not recover annotated boundaries: at ±0.5 s it loses to a speed
     threshold (0.183 vs 0.219) and over-segments 1.45×; its coverage advantage is bought at a
     price; boundary-level precision should use the threshold baseline.
   • The Kalman layer fits its noise at the numerical floor (𝑅 median 1.6×10−9 ), so the posterior
     collapses onto the last visible frame and understates occluded uncertainty; a trained ridge
     predictor beats it at every mask length (Table 7), so the filter is kept for its information-value
     derivation, and the value-of-information rule is record-independent (Section 9).
   • The test set is 7 records from one recording campaign, so no cross-operator or cross-site
     generalisation is claimed; Q3 uses the released synthetic jobs and a sample-average scenario
     approximation, not a guarantee.

   """P2 alternative module ( part 2) : ∗when∗ to spend a paid look inside a gap.


   `alt_p2_kalman.py` answers "how accurate is the damped constant− velocity
    filter " and scores the three official decision options A/B/C, which fix the
    prefix length up front .       This module answers the question those options leave
   open: once a gap is longer than the return delay , ∗∗where inside the gap∗∗
   should the look be spent , and does an adaptive trigger beat a fixed schedule?

   frames [u, u+6) and is usable only once u + 30 < W, so:


       W <= 31 frames (1.03 s) −> no interior look is possible at all

   whatever the policy ; the official options A/B/C get their information from a
   longer ∗ prefix ∗, not from a look inside the gap. Within−gap scheduling only
    exists for W >= 1.5 s , and that is where this module works.

     hold        never request ; predict the last observed position
     damped      never request ; damped constant− velocity Kalman forecast
      fixed       request at equally spaced instants inside the usable window

   and fixed (q, r ) the posterior position trace depends on the horizon only , not
   on the data , so such a rule fires either always or never −− it degenerates to a

   def drift_cm (obs, alpha , q, r , horizon) :

   def build_residual_bank (n_scen: int , split =" all ") :
       """Return bank[( prefix , family ) ] = sorted residuals , plus quantile grid .


        split =" all "           −> every eligible segment ( default , 343 units ; ALL THREE splits)
        split =" train "         −> train split only ( the conservative choice )
        split =" validation " −> validation split only : this is the pool the official
                                    engine samples true durations from, so its residuals are
                                     the correctly calibrated ones.
       """
       if split == " train " :
             tr = PRED[PRED.split == "train" ]. copy()
        elif split == " validation " :
             tr = PRED[PRED.split == "validation" ]. copy()
       else :
             tr = PRED[PRED.unit_id.isin(set(UNIT.unit_id)) ]. copy()
       tr [" resid "] = tr [" duration "] − tr ["pred"]
       bank = {}
       for p in PREFIX:
             sub = tr [np. isclose ( tr . prefix , p)]
             for fam in ("A", "L", "P") :
                  arr = np. sort (sub[sub. family == fam][" resid " ]. values )
                  if len ( arr ) < 8:
                         arr = np. sort (sub[" resid " ]. values )
                  bank[(p, fam)] = arr
       u = np. linspace (0.05, 0.95, n_scen)
       qbank = {k: np. quantile (v, u) for k, v in bank.items () }
       return qbank, u, {k: len (v) for k, v in bank.items () }



   def scenario_durations (job , level : int , qbank, n_scen: int , idx=None,
Team CSWBMCM2634892                                                                              Appendix, p. 38

                               alpha : float = 1.0, beta : float = 0.5) :
       """Duration scenarios ( alpha∗d+beta) at observation level .
       `` alpha ``/`` beta `` are the official    service −duration mapping; the nominal
       (1.0, 0.5) reproduces the original scenario set exactly .


       `idx` is the scenario −>quantile−rank assignment for THIS job. Using the same
       rank for every job (idx = arange , the default ) produces a ∗comonotone∗ scenario
       set : in scenario k every job sits at the same residual quantile , i . e . all jobs
       are simultaneously slow or simultaneously fast . That is a far harsher joint
       law than the protocol ' s (independent draws from the validation pool) and it
       makes every multi−job plan look infeasible in most scenarios , so the MIP drops
       jobs it could have run.       Passing an independent rank permutation per job turns
       the set into a Monte−Carlo sample of the joint law.
       """
       p = PREFIX[level]
       pred = float (PREDI.loc[(job[" unit " ], p) , "pred" ])
       q = qbank[(p, job["family" ]) ]
       if len (q) != n_scen:
             q = np. quantile (qbank[(p, job["family" ]) ], np. linspace (0.05, 0.95, n_scen))
       if idx is not None:
             q = np. asarray (q)[np. asarray (idx , dtype=int ) ]
       return [ alpha ∗ max(p, pred + float ( r ) ) + beta for r in q]



   def scen_idx(job , n_scen: int ) :
       """ Deterministic independent quantile −rank permutation for one job .


       Comonotone scenario sets (every job at the same rank in scenario k) model
       " all jobs slow together " , which is not the protocol ' s law. A per−job
       permutation keeps each job ' s marginal exact while making the cross−job
       dependence a random sample instead of the worst case .         Deterministic in the
       unit id so runs are reproducible .
       """
       h = zlib . crc32( str (job[" unit " ]) . encode(" utf −8")) & 0xFFFFFFFF
       rng = random.Random(h)
       rest = [ r for r in range(n_scen) if r != n_scen // 2]
       rng. shuffle ( rest )
       # Scenario 0 is reserved as the COMMIT trajectory: every job sits at its own
       # median there (rank n_scen //2) , so the admission test s_j + b_j <= H is
       # evaluated on a single coherent median schedule instead of on an arbitrary
       # mixed−rank scenario .
       return [n_scen // 2] + rest



   def median_qbank(qbank):
       """1−point scenario bank at the median residual ( for order−only planning ) . """
       return {k: np. array ([ float (np.median(v)) ]) for k, v in qbank.items () }



   def reserve_for (job , level : int , qbank, eps: float , n_scen: int ,
                      alpha : float = 1.0, beta : float = 0.5) :
       """Chance−constrained reserve : smallest b with P(p_j > b) <= eps. """
       vals = sorted ( scenario_durations (job , level , qbank, n_scen,
                                             alpha=alpha, beta=beta) )
       idx = int (math. ceil ((1.0 − eps) ∗ len ( vals ) ) ) − 1
       idx = min(max(idx, 0) , len ( vals ) − 1)
Team CSWBMCM2634892                                                                                            Appendix, p. 39

       return max(0.2, vals [idx ])

   # 2. Instance construction (same true durations as the official engine)
   # −−−−−−−−−−−−−−−−−−−−−−−−−−−−−−−−−−−−−−−−−−−−−−−−−−−−−−−−−−−−−−−−−−−−−−−−−−−−

     # scenario makes the whole chain obey the joint worst case ( all jobs slow at

     # room for only a handful of jobs .
     # Scenario 0 is the COMMIT trajectory built by scen_idx () : every job at its own
     # median residual , i . e . one coherent median schedule . The protocol admission
     # test s_j + b_j <= H is enforced only there .             Enforcing it in all scenarios

   def _paired_ci (by_key, seeds , case , a , b, key="weighted_on_time_rate") :
       d = []
       for s in seeds :
            ta , tb = by_key.get (( case , a , s) ) , by_key.get (( case , b, s) )
            if ta and tb :
                 d.append(ta [key] − tb [key])
       n = len (d)
       m = sum(d) / n

    real fitted model with train / validation / test discipline — not only a

   for parameter fitting , validation for model selection , test for evaluation ,

    linear model" as a baseline .       This script supplies exactly that , on the SAME
   clean prefix +mask windows and the SAME endpoint−error metric as the Kalman

       (6∗63 = 378 features ) to the endpoint DISPLACEMENT from the last visible

        strength is selected on the validation split from a 6−point grid , and a

        split is reported as an independent fitting − quality check.

        scalar a(L) fitted by least squares on train , prediction
            tgt = last_visible + a(L) ∗ ( last_visible − previous_visible ) .

   def _xy(obs, tgt ) :
         """ feature = flattened prefix ; target = displacement from last visible frame."""
         X = obs. reshape ( len (obs) , −1). astype (np. float64 )
         last = obs [:, −1, :]. reshape ( len (obs) , −1)
         Y = tgt . reshape ( len ( tgt ) , −1) − last
         return X, Y, last



   def _err_cm(pred_abs, tgt ) :
         e = np. linalg . norm(pred_abs.reshape (−1, 21, 3) − tgt . reshape (−1, 21, 3) , axis=−1)
         return e ∗ 100.0
