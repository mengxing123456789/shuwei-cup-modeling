Problem C：State Estimation and Decision Optimization for
Restocking Operations in Unmanned Supermarkets
1 Background
Machine vision, the Internet of Things and electronic payment are increasingly used in retail, bringing
together product recognition, convenient checkout and digital operations. Unmanned retail has potential in
communities, business parks and campuses. Its continued operation also depends on timely, reliable and
affordable restocking services.
Restocking involves repeated product arrangement, picking, placement and transport. Shelf edges,
operators’ hands and moving restocking carts may obstruct the view, while motion speed and operational
stages vary with the task and environment. Figure 1 illustrates possible occlusion in a restocking scene. A
system must assess the reliability of available information, decide when to observe again and schedule
subsequent work within limited time and resources.
Human operation records provide evidence for studying these questions. Observation quality means the
completeness and reliability of recorded information such as hand positions and orientations; missing fields,
occlusion and proximity to image edges provide relevant evidence. Actual motion, observation quality and
stage transitions can support models of state estimation and operational patterns, which can then inform
restocking decisions. Applying these patterns to robots requires explaining the relationships between hand
states and product states, and between human and robot operation durations, together with their conditions
of applicability.
Figure 1. Restocking scene and possible occlusion; illustrative rather than a measured layout of the source records.
Three sets of raw records, EgoDemo, EgoPro and EgoStandard, are provided, including video, 3D hand
trajectories, camera parameters, timing information, quality flags and semantic segments. Use the raw data
as the main evidence to study operational states and restocking decisions under observation uncertainty, and
develop an evidence-supported solution.
2 Problems
Problem 1. Modeling operational states and observation reliability
Restocking-related operations include reaching, picking, placement, transport and arrangement. Similar
operations may exhibit different motion patterns and information quality across environments, operational
stages and observation conditions.

Read and organize all three raw datasets, checking time alignment, coordinate conventions, duplicate
records and quality flags. A “record” means the operation data from one acquisition sequence; a “time
interval” is a continuous portion of a record. Use field completeness, temporal continuity and observation
quality to identify which records and intervals support model fitting, error evaluation, analysis of low-
quality observations or exclusion, and explain the reasons and selection criteria. Develop mathematical
models of changes in operational states and observation reliability, and investigate differences across tasks,
environments or stages.
Figure 2. Operational states and observation reliability in the raw records.
Figure 2 illustrates the relationship between operational states and observation quality. Select and define
your own state variables, such as hand positions, orientations or relative motion, and retain this
representation in Problem 2. Influencing factors are task, stage, environment and historical information that
may be associated with state changes or observation quality; select and test them using the records. Each
hand has 21 recorded 3D keypoints. A representative hand point may be a selected keypoint or the
geometric center of several keypoints; specify and justify its definition. A hand state must not be directly
interpreted as a product state.
Use the supplied raw records for both modeling and validation. Their correspondence is listed in
Data_Documentation/record_index.csv, and the environment split in Data_Documentation/record_split.csv.
Use the training set for parameter fitting, the validation set for model selection and the test set for
evaluation after freezing the model. Include at least one reproducible baseline with a simple structure and
few parameters, such as a constant predictor or a simple linear model, with its form appropriate to the state
definition. Report data retention, main parameters and their uncertainty, error or consistency checks, and
failure cases. Qualified held-out records may serve as references, but are not error-free, independent
physical ground truth.
Problem 2. State prediction and re-observation during missing observations
When a hand reaches deep into a shelf, its operational state may temporarily become difficult to observe.
Holding the last recorded state may introduce error, while frequent observation consumes resources. Study
how prediction error or uncertainty grows without new observations, how long prediction can continue
within justified tolerances, and when a new observation should be requested.
This problem requires decisions that balance prediction error and observation expenditure. Retain the state
variables selected and defined in Problem 1. Develop a prediction and uncertainty-update model for
missing-observation periods, analyze the effects of missing duration, operational stage and historical
information, and determine request times and waiting arrangements. Update your assessment as information
returns. Waiting means making no additional request or waiting for requested information to return;
prediction using existing information may continue. You may minimize observation cost and waiting
subject to error requirements, or improve predictive accuracy with given resources. Explain your objective,
tolerances and tradeoffs.

Figure 3. Prediction during missing observations and re-observation by replaying existing records.
Use artificial masking and replay experiments on real records: select intervals with qualified reference
states, temporarily hide selected times or channels, predict using only information available at that point,
and evaluate against the held-out records, as illustrated in Figure 3. A missing-observation window
specifies the start and end of hidden observations. Windows, observation budgets, per-request costs and
return delays are experimental conditions fixed in advance and shared by all compared methods. The
decisions are when to request observations and how to wait; prediction error, accumulated cost and waiting
time are evaluation measures. Make the experiments reproducible. Their results do not represent actual
robot performance.
Re-observation may return only times and channels that actually exist in the raw records, and returned
information cannot be used before its delay has elapsed. Compare against baselines such as holding the last
state, simple motion prediction or fixed-interval observation, reporting error, uncertainty and observation
expenditure. Use natural occlusion to analyze its occurrence, duration and recovery. Do not calculate true
position error for low-quality intervals without an independent reference. Explain differences between
artificial masking and natural occlusion; choosing arbitrary new viewpoints is not required. See
Data_Documentation/missing_observation_experiments.md for the experimental conventions.
Problem 3. Scheduling restocking operations under uncertainty
For the restocking-related jobs supplied in the attachments, use raw operation records to estimate operation
durations and their uncertainty. Within limited time and cost, determine which jobs to prioritize and in what
order, their reserved service durations, paid information reviews and waiting arrangements. Evaluate
completion, on-time service, cost and equipment occupancy.
Before execution, reserve a period of equipment service time for each job. This reserved service duration is
a decision, rather than a known realized duration. An overrun delays later jobs; if service finishes early, the
remainder of the reservation stays occupied and becomes idle time. Job order, reserved durations and the
choice to pay for reviewing existing information all affect subsequent scheduling.
Combine the first two problems into a scheduling model and apply it to the supplied task cases S1, S2 and
S3; no additional task list is needed. Figure 4 relates recorded segments to scenario jobs. One work unit
corresponds to one text-annotated interval, rather than one product. Of 349 intervals, the 343 lasting at least
1.2 seconds enter common sampling; the remaining intervals stay in the source index. The cases contain 8,
12 and 16 jobs, with horizons H of 100, 100 and 125 seconds, cost budgets of 32, 34 and 40, and paid-
review limits of 4, 6 and 8, respectively. Costs use abstract units.
Restocking_Tasks_and_Constraints/tasks.csv lists each job’s release time (earliest availability), due time
(desired completion time), weight (relative importance), service zone and predecessors (jobs that must
finish before execution). A job may finish after its due time but within H, with tardiness recorded. H
terminates the entire trial. Field definitions and examples are provided in the attachment guide.

Figure 4. Operation records and scenario job conditions.
Upon release, a job provides the first 0.2 seconds of numerical data from the start of its source segment,
free of charge. Each job allows at most two paid reviews: the first extends the cumulative visible prefix to
0.6 seconds and the second to 1.0 second. Each review consumes 1 scenario second and 1 cost unit; waiting
does not extend the prefix. The 0.2, 0.6 and 1.0 seconds are cumulative windows in the original segment,
whereas review delays use scheduling time. Review means graded disclosure of existing demonstration
information, rather than a new physical viewpoint. Unrevealed source identifiers, end times and subsequent
states cannot be used in current decisions.
Decisions include which jobs to prioritize and their order, each reserved service duration, whether and when
to pay for review, how long to wait and when to stop. Figure 5 illustrates these relationships. On-time
completion, tardiness, accumulated cost, overrun and reserved idle time are evaluation measures. Choose
your research focus and optimization objective, justify weights and tradeoffs, and report all common
metrics for every method.
Common constraints allow each job to execute at most once and prohibit parallel execution, review and
waiting. Respect releases, precedence, time and cost limits. If the original annotation duration is d, the
scenario service duration is αd + β. The nominal mapping uses α = 1 and β = 0.5 seconds. Test sensitivity
with α = 0.75 or 1.5 while β remains 0.5 seconds, and with β = 0 or 1 second while α remains 1. Estimate
duration models and their uncertainty from the raw records and evaluate them on held-out records.
The reserved service duration b must be at least 0.2 seconds. For inter-zone distance D, movement and
changeover take D + 0.5 seconds, and execution costs 2 + 0.1b + 0.2D. Check the budget and reserved
interval before execution. If realized scenario service takes p and starts at s, completion occurs at s + p, and
the equipment becomes available again at s + max(p, b). If service crosses horizon H, terminate the trial and
count that job as unfinished. Account for all actions and costs according to the attached rules.
Figure 5. Job order, information review and service reservations under limited resources.
Environments are identified by environment_id in the original metadata; one environment may contain
multiple records. Training, validation and test sets contain 16, 4 and 4 environments, for parameter fitting,
model selection and evaluation of frozen solutions. A random seed is an integer initial value that controls
sampling and permits replication. For each case and mapping condition, use the same set of 30 supplied
seeds to form 30 trials; all methods receive identical sampled jobs for paired comparison. Analyze
performance, variation and parameter sensitivity. Objectives are open. If cost or capacity risk is the primary
objective, the mean weighted on-time completion rate for each case under nominal conditions must be at
least 0.50; report any failure to meet this requirement. See
Restocking_Tasks_and_Constraints/tasks_and_evaluation.md for common metrics and the complete
protocol.

Task demands, costs, zones and duration mappings are scenario assumptions, rather than robot parameters
identified from the raw data. Zone coordinates are not product positions. Overrun represents capacity and
information risk, rather than grasp failure or collision. Common results come from a record-driven
scheduling scenario; splicing original videos into a new order cannot demonstrate actual robot performance
in that order.

EgoDemo uses JSON, Parquet and MP4 files; the other two datasets use MCAP containers with embedded
message schemas. Check field meanings, frame indices and coordinate directions before harmonizing time
and units. World-coordinate origins from different records do not define a shared store layout. The head
stereo cameras have camera parameters; world-frame extrinsics for wrist cameras are unavailable, so four
video views cannot be assumed to share a common calibration.
Original quality flags concern human hands, with reasons including occlusion and near edge. They do not
directly indicate product occlusion or operation success. Numerically complete states may still be low
quality. Semantic segments mainly contain natural-language descriptions with task-dependent granularity;
they do not automatically correspond to individual product operations.
Do not randomly split adjacent frames, records or related samples from the same environment and then
treat them as independent training and test samples. Environments overlap across the three datasets. All
three problems use the environment split in Data_Documentation/record_split.csv, also checking scene and
operator overlap. The copy in Restocking_Tasks_and_Constraints is identical. Fix models, thresholds,
weights and preferences before viewing test results. Report source differences and excluded samples
separately. Operators may appear in multiple environments, so this split does not establish validation on
entirely unseen operators.
Compared methods must use identical information, budgets, delays and access conditions. Problem 3 uses
the fixed attached replay protocol; in Problem 2, specify missing-observation and re-observation conditions
and apply them consistently to every comparator. When hiding a reference state, also hide state features
calculated from it. Future trajectories, future segment endpoints and information that has not returned
cannot enter current decisions. A query does not make low-quality coordinates an accurate reference.
4 Common evaluation metrics and comparisons
Open objectives express the research focus, while common metrics permit verification of results. For
Problem 1, report retained record and timestamp counts, the data split, model parameters, state error or
consistency, quality-model performance and uncertainty checks. Match metrics to state definitions and do
not summarize only easy samples.

For Problem 2, report prediction error, performance at different missing durations, interval coverage or set
reliability, observation counts and cost, waiting time and computational expense. For a representative 3D

success rate without physical robot experiments.
Use identical records, jobs, information and resources for comparisons, and analyze the contributions of
state models, information arrangements and job order. Aggregate raw-data validation by record or
environment. For Problem 3, summarize paired trials by task case and report differences and variation.
Explain the scale, weights and basis of custom objectives; scores defined by different teams or preferences
cannot be compared directly by their numerical size.
5 Attachments and submission requirements
See Data_Documentation/attachment_guide.md for the complete inventory and field meanings.
Problem_Statement contains matching English Word and PDF files and a preserved Chinese reference PDF.
Raw_Data contains the three complete local data packages, EgoDemo.zip, EgoPro.zip and EgoStandard.zip.
English packaging changes directory names and accompanying notes only; recorded payloads remain
unchanged. Use file_manifest.csv at the package root to verify all attachments.
Data_Documentation provides the source index for 69 records, a raw-field dictionary, the shared record
split, artificial-masking conventions, data provenance and publication references. Read the original files and
perform your own cleaning, variable selection, parameter estimation and modeling. Any programming
language may be used.
Restocking_Tasks_and_Constraints provides the common jobs, service zones, sampling families, the index
of 349 source intervals, record split, common_conditions.json and tasks_and_evaluation.md, defining
information access, time and cost accounting, and common evaluation rules.
Hand trajectories describe human operational states, and quality flags describe hand observations. Product
geometry, spatial occupancy and robot performance require corresponding independent references. Clearly
label proxy experiments and scenario-based analysis.
Submit a research report, reproducible programs and main result files, explaining the model basis,
validation, applicability and limitations. For Problem 3, also submit frozen models and decision rules, trial-
level results under common conditions and process logs, as specified in
Restocking_Tasks_and_Constraints/tasks_and_evaluation.md. Distinguish real-record analysis, artificial-
masking tests, annotation validation and scenario trials.
