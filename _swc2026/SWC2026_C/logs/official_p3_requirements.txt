For the supplied restocking-related jobs, estimate operation durations and their uncertainty from the original 
records. Under the common time, cost and information constraints, determine job selection and order, 
reserved service durations, paid information reviews, waiting and stopping decisions. Participants choose and 
justify their optimization objective and report every common metric below.

2  Data split and permitted information
Use the supplied record_split.csv for all three problems: 16 training, 4 validation and 4 test environments. 
Keep all records and intervals from one environment together. Fit on training data, select models and 
thresholds on validation data, and freeze models and decision rules before testing. Operators may occur 
across environments; this is not entirely unseen-operator validation.
Reset policy state for each trial. Public task conditions and information already returned during that trial are 
available to the policy. Returned execution feedback may update current assessments. Do not accumulate 
validation/test source truths as fitting data for later trials. Do not use the full source collection, source index, 
prefix matching, seed knowledge or hidden source identifiers to retrieve unrevealed states, endpoints or 
durations. All source records are public for checking; compliance must be demonstrated by reproducible 
programs and logs.
On release, a job supplies its first 0.2 source seconds of numerical data free of charge. Each job permits at 
most two paid reviews: the first extends the cumulative visible prefix to 0.6 seconds, the second to 1.0 
second. Each costs 1 unit and occupies the equipment for 1 scenario second, also subject to K and B. Return 
time may not exceed H. Only released, unattempted jobs may be reviewed; completed predecessors are not 
required for review. Information returns only after the delay. Waiting alone reveals no additional data.
Prefixes begin at the original annotation start and contain only existing samples within the window. They 
include timestamps; positions and orientations of both hands’ 21 keypoints; head and left/right head-camera 
poses; and original hand-quality flags. They exclude source identifiers, segment endpoints, total duration,

3  Duration mapping and feasible actions
Scenario service duration is p = αd + β. Nominal conditions are α = 1 and β = 0.5 seconds. Sensitivity 
conditions are α = 0.75 and 1.5 with β = 0.5, and β = 0 and 1 second with α = 1. These are five conditions, 
not a full factorial design. They are problem assumptions, not measured robot parameters.
Each job executes at most once. Execution requires release and completed predecessors. The predecessors 
field contains job IDs separated by |; blank means none. release_s is earliest information availability, due_s is 
desired completion time, priority_weight is relative importance, and zone_id is the scenario service location. 
Late completion within H is allowed and incurs tardiness. No unsupported failure/retry mechanism is 
imposed.
There is one equipment unit. Execution, review and waiting cannot overlap. Waiting must be positive, cannot 
cross H and is free. Stopping is allowed at any time. At most 200 actions may be submitted per trial. Waiting 
and review do not change location.
Zones are Z0=(0,0), Z1=(1,0), Z2=(0,1), Z3=(1,1), Z4=(2,0). Let D be Euclidean distance from the current to 
the service zone. Travel speed is 1 length unit per second. Every execution adds 0.5 seconds of changeover, 
including execution in the current zone.
Execution selects a reserved service duration b ≥ 0.2 seconds. At current time t, service starts at s = t + D + 
0.5 and costs 2 + 0.1b + 0.2D. Before accepting the action, require cumulative cost ≤ B and s + b ≤ H. Do not 
use unrevealed p in this check. Charge an accepted execution immediately, with no refund. b excludes 
travel/changeover and is a decision, not a known realized duration or a start time.
Completion occurs at C = s + p. If C ≤ H, the job completes and the equipment is next available at s + 
max(p,b). A reservation is not released early: b > p creates reserved idle time; p > b extends occupancy. 
Execution is non-preemptive. If H arrives before service finishes, terminate at H and count the executed job 
as unfinished and censored. The trial also ends when the equipment next becomes available at H. Log 
executed-source references after completion or termination; later information cannot revise an earlier 
decision.
An unreleased or repeated job, unmet predecessor, insufficient reservation, excess cost, a planned action 
crossing H, non-finite or otherwise invalid numerical values, or exceeding 200 actions is an infeasible 
command. Do not implement it. Retain resources already consumed, record a violation and terminate the 
trial. Keep violating trials in the reported results.

5  Submission requirements
Submit the research report, reproducible programs, frozen models and decision rules, environment 
information, and validation results for all five mappings. Each trial-result row identifies case, split, trial 
number, seed, α, β and all common metrics. Use the metric names above or clearly defined equivalents. 
Summaries must trace to trial-level records.
Process logs identify decision time, action, job, available information, reservation or waiting duration, return 
time, and changes in time, zone and cost. For execution, include service start, completion, overrun, idle and 
censoring. Add executed-source references for retrospective checking. Logs must permit checking 
information access and task/resource feasibility. Programming language and implementation are unrestricted.
The task, price, zone and duration-mapping values define a record-driven scenario. Zone coordinates are not 
measured product positions. Overrun is capacity risk, not grasp failure or collision. These results do not 
establish physical robot performance.
