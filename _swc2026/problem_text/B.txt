I. Background
When
small
Unmanned
Aerial
Vehicles
(UAVs)
appear
in
low-altitude
airspace,
reconnaissance systems can only generate coarse alert information containing the target’s
approximate azimuth, horizontal distance, and altitude. Such information alone cannot yield the
target’s precise geolocation. To enable rapid direction-finding and localization, the dispatch center
dispatches ground-based mobile observation vehicles (hereafter observation vehicles) fitted with
gimbal-mounted cameras to vantage positions for target bearing measurement.
A single observation vehicle yields only one bearing line and cannot determine the target’s
horizontal position. Two vehicles observing synchronously from distinct azimuths can localize the
target via the intersection of two bearing lines. The target altitude is provided directly by the alert
message and is not solved through intersection. It constrains whether the line-of- sight elevation
falls within the gimbal’s allowable range, determines whether buildings occlude the line-of- sight,
and influences subsequent pointing- error analysis.
A too-short baseline between the two stations, or an excessively large or small intersection
angle, will amplify localization errors. Consequently, station geometry directly governs
intersection-based positioning accuracy. During deployment, vehicles are constrained by building
obstructions, line-of- sight occlusion, and arrival-time deadlines. New alerts may arrive
unexpectedly, while vehicle failures or communication outages can also occur. This problem
addresses core questions: which vehicles to select, where to place observation stations, how one
single vehicle can sequentially serve multiple alerts, and how to reschedule tasks after
disturbances take place.

Vehicle, Camera- Gimbal, and Observability Conditions
Observation vehicle Each observation vehicle is modeled as a disk with an equivalent
radius of 1.5 m. Vehicles must not cross the field boundary or enter building interiors.
The constant travel speed is 3 m/s. Acceleration, deceleration, vehicle steering, and
collision- avoidance time are neglected.

--- PAGE 3 ---
Camera and gimbal The camera The camera is mounted at the geometric center of the
vehicle’s equivalent disk, with a height of 2 m above ground. The feasible gimbal
elevation range is [ −10∘, 80∘]. Both the pan and tilt axes have a maximum angular
speed of 30∘/s and may rotate simultaneously. The slewing duration is determined by
the longer rotation time of the two axes. While the vehicle is moving, the gimbal
remains pointed due east in the horizontal attitude. The gimbal slews toward the target
only after the vehicle has come to a complete stop.
Observability conditions The line-of- sight elevation angle must lie within the gimbal’s
feasible elevation range, and the line-of- sight shall be free from building occlusion.
Any tangency or contact between the line-of- sight segment and any edge, face, or
vertex of a building prism counts as occlusion.
Observation and reset rules After all vehicles assigned to one mission arrive at their
stations and finish gimbal pointing, a 5 s
joint observation is initiated. Upon
observation completion, the gimbal resets to the due-east horizontal attitude following
the angular-speed rule, and vehicles proceed to the next observation station.
Calculation baseline All computations adopt nominal parameters, except for the
error-case scenarios specified in Problem 3.
Full Timing Sequence for a Single Two- Vehicle Intersection Observation Mission
(1) Travel The vehicle drives from its current position to the observation station. During
travel, the gimbal maintains the due-east horizontal attitude.
(2) Gimbal slew After the vehicle arrives and stops at the station, the gimbal rotates to align
with the target direction.
(3) Synchronized observation After both vehicles assigned to the same alert complete
gimbal pointing, a joint observation is performed.
(4) Gimbal reset After observation ends, the gimbal resets to the due-east horizontal
attitude in accordance with gimbal speed rules. If the gimbal is already in the due-east
horizontal attitude, the rotation angle for reset equals zero and the reset duration is zero.
(5) Depart for next mission Immediately after gimbal reset finishes, the vehicle drives
toward the next observation station.
Note: The time consumed by every step is recorded into each vehicle’s time axis.
